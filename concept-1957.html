Solidarity Concept Analysis — UNGA 1957 (narrated report)

Study: projects/ungdc/studies/solidarity_concept_1957/ · Date: 2026-08-14 Corpus: 71 UNGA General Debate speeches, session 12, 1957. What this is: a concept-centered analysis of how "solidarity" is used — distinct from the sibling self-ascription/identity study. Unit = the concept and its semantic field. Method: term/passage-first extraction → machine-verified catalogue → federated L1–L4 abduction (typology of solidarity-use types, L4 stability 7/7 = 100%) → network.

The headline

In 1957, the literal word "solidarity" is rare; the concept travels through its semantic field — and it is overwhelmingly institutional and bloc-bound.

  • 22 passages use the literal word "solidarity" (in 16 of 71 speeches).
  • 54 passages invoke the concept through neighbors: "collective security" (22×), "fraternal/fellowship", "common cause", "mutual assistance", "brotherhood", "family of nations/peoples", "we the peoples".
  • Of the 76 concept passages, 65 are genuine solidarity claims (8 no-claims — mostly false-positive triggers and quoted third parties; 3 unclear).

So the first finding is about lexical density: a state in 1957 almost never says "solidarity" out loud. When it wants the concept, it reaches for the machinery of collective security and the language of kinship.

The Cold War signature

The most analytically interesting tension emerged during coding: both blocs invoke "institutional collective security" to legitimize their own arrangements. The typology's F1-vs-F2 boundary rule captures it:

  • F1 (universal/aspirational): RUS and YUG frame collective security as a non-bloc, anti-bloc, or UN-Charter ideal; NPL/PAN/ARG treat the UN/SC as the container of solidarity.
  • F2 (named pact + named adversary): TUR/USA/GBR/NLD legitimize NATO/Baghdad Pact/ SEATO against the Soviet threat; POL/HUN do the same for the Warsaw system.

Instruments confirm it: United Nations (22), NATO (8), collective security (6), UN Charter (5), Warsaw Treaty (4), Baghdad Pact (4). Both superpower blocs and the institutional UN are the vehicles through which 1957 states perform solidarity.

Kinship communities

F3 (26 claims, 16 states) is the largest family and splits into distinct identity- communities:

  • Arab "one people" fraternity — Lebanon (6 claims, incl. solidarity with Syria), plus LBY, SAU, SYR, IRQ.
  • Socialist "fraternal family of nations" — POL(2), ALB, UKR (fraternal bond vs fascism), distinct from Warsaw-defence framing (F2).
  • Afro-Asian / Bandung fellowship — LBN(3), the new postcolonial coalition.
  • "Family of nations / peoples" belonging — MYS, PAK, PER, MAR (new-member and decolonization welcome).
  • Civilizational/regional heritage — ESP(4, Europe/Mediterranean/civilization).

This is the relational-affective register of solidarity in 1957 — it binds identity- communities (Arab, socialist, Afro-Asian, civilizational) rather than UN machinery.

Claim direction & dimension

  • Direction: to_community=25, to_group=18, self_affiliation=14, to_other=7, to_category=1. Solidarity is mostly toward the UN/international community and toward groups/alliances; self-affiliation (belonging) is the third largest.
  • Dimension: community_boundary=23, voluntary_institutionalized=20, action_commitment=12, shared_predicament=5, relational_empathy=5. The Kornprobst–Senn markers boundary and institutionalized dominate — consistent with the institutional + bloc reading.

Who is silent?

Only 3 states make zero solidarity yes-claims: HND, IND, ROU. (Note: this is distinct from the identity study, where 12 great powers claimed nothing. Here IND — the connective tissue of the identity network — happens to use no solidarity concept passage in 1957; its solidarity appears as identity/self-affiliation, caught by the sibling study, not this one.) The concept is broadly distributed across 36 states.
