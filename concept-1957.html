Solidarity Concept Analysis — UNGA 1957 (narrated report)

Study: projects/ungdc/studies/solidarity_concept_1957/ · Date: 2026-08-14 Corpus: 71 UNGA General Debate speeches, session 12, 1957. What this is: a concept-centered analysis of how "solidarity" is used — distinct from the sibling self-ascription/identity study. Unit = the concept and its semantic field. Method: term/passage-first extraction → machine-verified catalogue → federated L1–L4 abduction (typology of solidarity-use types, L4 stability 7/7 = 100%).

The headline

In 1957, the literal word "solidarity" is rare; the concept travels through its semantic field — and it is overwhelmingly institutional and bloc-bound.

  • 22 passages use the literal word "solidarity" (in 16 of 71 speeches).
  • 54 passages invoke the concept through neighbors: "collective security" (22×), "fraternal/fellowship", "common cause", "mutual assistance", "brotherhood", "family of nations/peoples", "we the peoples".
  • Of the 76 concept passages, 65 are genuine solidarity claims (8 no-claims — mostly false-positive triggers and quoted third parties; 3 unclear).

So the first finding is about lexical density: a state in 1957 almost never says "solidarity" out loud. When it wants the concept, it reaches for the machinery of collective security and the language of kinship.

Section 1 — Actual "solidarity" mentions first (Layer A)

We begin with the literal word itself: 22 passages in 16 of 71 speeches. Every one of these 22 is a genuine solidarity claim — the word is never used idly in 1957. The speakers are: ALB, ARG, DOM, ECU, ESP(2), FRA(2), IRN, ITA(2), LAO(2), LBN, PAN, POL(2), PRY, SLV, SYR, VEN(2).

What does the literal word do when it is used? The 22 mentions distribute across the solidarity types:

Two observations stand out from Layer A alone:

  • Kinship (F3) dominates even the literal word — 8 of 22 mentions name "solidarity" as a bounded community bond: inter-American solidarity (DOM, ECU), Arab solidarity (LBN→Syria, SYR), socialist solidarity (POL). Where the word is spoken, it most often binds a community.
  • "International solidarity" is the recurrent phrase for institutional/development uses — "the United Nations has achieved positive results which demonstrate international solidarity" (ARG), "a general movement for international solidarity" (FRA), "international solidarity … bringing to us the benefit of their experience, technology and friendship" (LAO), "as extensive as is its sense of international solidarity" (PRY).

Not a single 1957 speech uses the literal word to claim universal-human solidarity (only VEN/LAO touch it, in the development/universal register). The word itself is used relationally — toward a community, the UN, a donor, or a standard — never as an abstract human ideal. That is the sharpest Layer-A finding.

Section 2 — Expanding to the semantic field (Layer B)

Because the literal word is rare, we expand to 54 passages where the concept is carried by neighbor terms (seeded lexicon + children flagging unflagged solidarity-performances):

Term Count
collective security 22
fraternal 6
fellowship 5
common cause 4
mutual assistance 3
common destiny / brotherhood 3 each
family of nations / peoples 3
we the peoples / we are one / mutual aid / stand with 1 each

The semantic field does not merely mirror Layer A — it flips its composition.

  • Collective security (22×) is the single dominant vehicle — it carries the institutional (F1) and defensive-alliance (F2) registers that the literal word almost never names. Layer A had only 4 institutional + 1 alliance mention; Layer B adds the other 12 F1 + 7 F2 claims.
  • Kinship (F3) is further reinforced via fraternity/fellowship/common cause/kin language (the Arab, socialist, Afro-Asian, and "family of nations" communities).
  • The empathetic (F4) and universal (F6) registers appear largely here too — fraternal sympathy (ESP), "human brotherhood" across distance (SAU), common destiny (MEX).

So: the literal word skews relational-community (kinship + institutional); the semantic field supplies the bloc-defensive, empathetic, and universal registers the word itself avoids. Together they give the full picture in the typology below.

Section 4 — Solidarity in evidence: quotes & context by type

Each type below is illustrated with the verbatim quote (and its context) drawn from the machine-verified records. Quotes are literal substrings of the 1957 transcripts.

F3 — Kinship-community solidarity (26 claims, 16 states)

The concept binds a bounded identity-community. "Inter-American solidarity":

"our policy in the United Nations is guided by the principles of inter-American solidarity" — DOM, claiming membership in the inter-American family. "a long-standing tradition of solidarity in matters of labour production, the mobilization of resources" — ECU, on the OAS states' economic ties.

Arab solidarity:

"Lebanon feels the deepest sense of solidarity with Syria" — LBN, on the Arab joint- defence pact with a sister state. "the solidarity existing between the Arabs is such that it cannot be broken by foreign efforts" — SYR.

Socialist fraternity:

"Strong and lasting bonds of solidarity link us with the other socialist countries, bonds forged by common needs" — POL.

Civilizational kinship:

"Between that world and the coasts of Spain there lies a common sea" — ESP, evoking centuries of Mediterranean/Near-East ties.

F1 — Institutional collective security (16 claims, 13 states)

Solidarity as the UN/Charter/collective-security machinery, universal or aspirational.

"the United Nations has achieved positive results which demonstrate international solidarity" — ARG. "replace the policy of mutually-antagonistic military blocs by a general system of collective security" — ALB. "can be assured only within that framework of international solidarity of which the United Nations is the living symbol" — IRN.

F2 — Defensive-alliance solidarity (8 claims, 7 states)

Solidarity as a bounded alliance against a named adversary.

"It has found its security in Atlantic solidarity and it remains strongly attached to the North Atlantic alliance" — ITA. "brought into being to provide a feeling of collective security against what we consider [the Soviet threat]" — GBR. "were forced to conclude the Warsaw Treaty, which safeguards our country's security until such time as an effective system of collective security" — POL.

F5 — Development-aid solidarity (4 claims, 4 states)

Solidarity operationalized as concrete material help, donor→recipient.

"in a magnificent gesture of international solidarity, are bringing to us the benefit of their experience, technology and friendship" — LAO, thanking donors. "making available to us the benefits of Brazil's seacoast, as extensive as is its sense of international solidarity" — PRY, thanking Brazil. "developing their economies within the framework of a general movement for international solidarity" — FRA, on real assistance (not arms).

F6 — Universal solidarity (6 claims, 5 states)

Solidarity as an abstract, non-exclusionary human ideal or common destiny.

"Modern diplomacy is inspired by a new concept of human solidarity" — VEN. "potential enemies were now inescapably bound by a common destiny, and must live or die together" — MEX, on the nuclear threat. "making common cause with those who, like ourselves, are dedicated to the quest for a just and equitable international order" — IRN.

F4 — Empathetic solidarity (3 claims, 3 states)

Sympathy/moral identification with an oppressed other, across distance.

"The Albanian people express their solidarity with the people of Syria and condemn the aggressive policies [of the US]" — ALB. "they do not doubt the fraternal sympathy of Spain" — ESP, for the Middle East/North Africa. "that Continent which is far removed from the scene of events but which is still so close because of its spirit of human brotherhood" — SAU, on Latin American protests against French actions in Algeria.

F7 — Normative solidarity (2 claims, 2 states)

Solidarity used as a criterion of "true" vs artificial solidarity, or a self-ascribed trait.

"groups are formed within it that are justified neither by geography nor true solidarity" — FRA, condemning artificial blocs. "has at all times distinguished itself by its spirit of solidarity and by its support of the great moral and legal principles" — SLV, self-ascribing a solidarity trait.

Section 5 — The Cold War signature

The most analytically interesting tension emerged during coding: both blocs invoke "institutional collective security" to legitimize their own arrangements. The typology's F1-vs-F2 boundary rule captures it:

  • F1 (universal/aspirational): RUS and YUG frame collective security as a non-bloc, anti-bloc, or UN-Charter ideal; NPL/PAN/ARG treat the UN/SC as the container of solidarity.
  • F2 (named pact + named adversary): TUR/USA/GBR/NLD legitimize NATO/Baghdad Pact/ SEATO against the Soviet threat; POL/HUN do the same for the Warsaw system.

Instruments confirm it: United Nations (22), NATO (8), collective security (6), UN Charter (5), Warsaw Treaty (4), Baghdad Pact (4). Both superpower blocs and the institutional UN are the vehicles through which 1957 states perform solidarity.

Section 6 — Claim direction & dimension

  • Direction: to_community=25, to_group=18, self_affiliation=14, to_other=7, to_category=1. Solidarity is mostly toward the UN/international community and toward groups/alliances; self-affiliation (belonging) is the third largest.
  • Dimension: community_boundary=23, voluntary_institutionalized=20, action_commitment=12, shared_predicament=5, relational_empathy=5. The Kornprobst–Senn markers boundary and institutionalized dominate — consistent with the institutional + bloc reading.

Section 7 — Who is silent?

Only 3 states make zero solidarity yes-claims: HND, IND, ROU. (Note: this is distinct from the identity study, where 12 great powers claimed nothing. Here IND — the connective tissue of the identity network — happens to use no solidarity concept passage in 1957; its solidarity appears as identity/self-affiliation, caught by the sibling study, not this one.) The concept is broadly distributed across 36 states.
